AI In Oncology Drug Development: Adoption To Expand While
Clinical Validation Remains A Constraint
Key View
• Pharmaceutical companies will expand their use of licensed biological AI models as
oncology research tools.
• FDA–EMA coordination will clarify how pharmaceutical companies prepare AI-
supported evidence for drug submissions.
• AI-supported oncology discovery will face a prolonged period of clinical validation
despite establishing a sizeable pipeline.
Pharmaceutical companies will expand their use of licensed biological artificial
intelligence (AI) models as oncology research tools. Existing arrangements range
from paying AI companies to identify drug targets to licensing broader research
platforms and jointly developing biological models. Under AstraZeneca's April 2025
agreement with Tempus and Pathos, Pathos is developing an oncology model using
Tempus's patient data for subsequent use by all three partners. By contrast, GSK's
January 2026 agreement with Noetik allows its researchers to use existing models to
investigate tumour biology in non-small cell lung cancer and colorectal cancer without
building the models themselves. The five-year, non-exclusive agreement provides
access in return for annual licensing fees. This offers a scalable business model for
biological AI, in which developers can license the same trained model to multiple
pharmaceutical companies rather than relying solely on bespoke partnerships. We
expect further agreements to favour companies with differentiated cancer datasets, as
these underpin models' ability to simulate tumour biology and support drug discovery.
Noetik's detailed data on individual cells and their surrounding tumour environments
illustrate this potential advantage, allowing the company to use its proprietary datasets
across model licensing, commissioned research and its own drug-development
programmes.
FDA–EMA coordination will clarify how pharmaceutical companies prepare AI-
supported evidence for drug submissions. Pharmaceutical companies are
increasingly using AI to analyse complex clinical, real-world and other drug-
development data, increasing the need for clarity over the reliability of AI-supported
results. On January 14 2026, the FDA and European Medicines Agency published 10
shared principles intended to guide future regulation across the drug's life cycle. The
principles call for companies to define how AI will generate evidence on a drug's quality,

Drug Regulators Are Reviewing A Growing Volume Of AI-Supported Submissions
AI-supported oncology discovery will face a prolonged period of clinical validation despite establishing a sizeable
pipeline. AI-supported drug discovery has moved beyond laboratory research and produced a measurable clinical pipeline. An
industry-wide analysis published in Cancer Treatment Reviews in October 2026 identified 129 AI-enabled candidates that had
entered human trials across 69 companies by March 1 2026, with oncology accounting for 74 candidates, or 57.3% of the
total. These findings establish substantial clinical entry, but the pipeline's current concentration in early trials means that
there is still limited evidence on whether these candidates will provide treatment benefits. An August 2026 Cancer Medicine
review highlights further obstacles to clinical translation. AI predictions based mainly on laboratory data may prove less
reliable when tested against the greater complexity of cancers in patients, while AI-designed molecules may not always be
practical to manufacture. Near-to-medium-term progress will therefore depend on companies identifying unsuitable
predictions earlier, before committing substantial capital to clinical development. A recent commercial example is the April
2026 partnership between Crown Bioscience and Turbine, which integrates AI prediction with patient-derived tumour testing
to address the gap between computational promise and clinical translation. More extensive testing should improve candidate
selection but will retain significant time and cost for discovery.

Haematological Cancer
CHMP Recommends Approval Of Talvey And Daratumumab Combination For Earlier-Line
Multiple Myeloma In Europe
The Committee for Medicinal Products for Human Use (CHMP) of the European Medicines Agency has recommended
approval of Johnson & Johnson’s Talvey (talquetamab) in combination with daratumumab, with or without pomalidomide, for
adults with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma who have received at least one prior therapy. This regimen, which targets
GPRC5D and CD38, would be the first bispecific antibody combination available as early as second-line treatment. Data from
the Phase 3 MonumenTAL-3 trial showed progression-free survival rates of up to 81% and overall survival rates of up to 89% at
24 months. The safety profile was consistent with previously reported results for the individual therapies. The proposed
combination may expand treatment options for patients with early relapse and support Johnson & Johnson’s position in the
European multiple myeloma market.
Genito-Urinary Cancer
FDA Accepts NDA For UroGen's UGN-103 Bladder Cancer Therapy
The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has accepted the New Drug Application (NDA) for UGN-103, UroGen Pharma's
investigational intravesical therapy designed for adults with recurrent low-grade intermediate-risk non-muscle invasive
bladder cancer (LG-IR-NMIBC). The submission is supported by data from the Phase 3 UTOPIA trial, which demonstrated a
77.8% complete response rate at three months and sustained response at six months. UGN-103 utilises UroGen's proprietary
RTGel technology and incorporates manufacturing modifications to improve clinical administration. If approved, UGN-103
would complement UroGen's existing ZUSDURI therapy, with intellectual property protection expected until 2044. The FDA
has set a Prescription Drug User Fee Act target action date of June 2027.

Gastrointestinal Cancer
CHMP Issues Positive Opinion For RASONQUE In Metastatic Pancreatic Cancer
The European Medicines Agency’s Committee for Medicinal Products for Human Use (CHMP) has issued a positive opinion
recommending marketing authorisation for ‘RASONQUE’ (daraxonrasib), an oral RAS inhibitor developed by Revolution
Medicines, for adults with metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma who have previously received systemic therapy. The
recommendation is based on results from the global Phase 3 RASolute 302 study, which demonstrated a reduction in the risk
of death and improvements in both overall survival and progression-free survival compared with chemotherapy, irrespective
of RAS mutation status. Subject to European Commission approval, ‘RASONQUE’ would be the first broadly RAS-targeted
therapy for this indication in the EU, addressing a population with limited treatment options. The CHMP’s positive opinion
follows recent US Food and Drug Administration approval and highlights the role of accelerated regulatory pathways in
enabling access to innovative oncology treatments.
Head and Neck Cancer
BioNTech Reports No Statistically Significant Progression-Free Survival Benefit For BNT113 In
Phase II/III HPV16-Positive Head And Neck Cancer Trial
BioNTech has announced results from the Phase II/III AHEAD-MERIT trial evaluating BNT113, its investigational messenger
RNA (mRNA) cancer immunotherapy, in combination with pembrolizumab for patients with unresectable, recurrent or
metastatic human papillomavirus type 16 (HPV16)-positive head and neck squamous cell carcinoma. Interim analysis showed
no statistically significant improvement in progression-free survival for the combination therapy compared with
pembrolizumab alone. The trial will continue to assess overall survival, which remains a primary endpoint. BNT113 targets
HPV16-derived antigens and is part of BioNTech’s oncology pipeline, which is focused on unmet clinical needs in HPV-
associated head and neck cancer. The therapy has received US FDA Fast Track Designation and continues under evaluation as
part of the company’s mRNA-based immunotherapy strategy.
General Developments
Marengo Therapeutics Receives Second US FDA Fast Track Designation For Invikafusp Alfa In
TMB-H Solid Tumours
Marengo Therapeutics has received a second US FDA Fast Track Designation for its investigational immuno-oncology agent,
invikafusp alfa. The designation broadens eligibility from tumor mutational burden-high (TMB-H) colorectal cancer to a tissue-
agnostic indication for unresectable, locally advanced or metastatic TMB-H solid tumours that have progressed following
previous therapies, including immune checkpoint inhibitors. The expanded designation is supported by monotherapy
responses across multiple tumour types in the ongoing STARt-001 trial. Invikafusp alfa is the first T-cell agonist to obtain Fast
Track status in a biomarker-defined, tissue-agnostic context. This regulatory development is based on Phase II clinical data,
with further updates expected at the European Society for Medical Oncology Congress.

CHMP Issues Positive Opinion On Extended Dosing Schedule For Libtayo In EU Cancer
Indications
The European Medicines Agency Committee for Medicinal Products for Human Use (CHMP) has recommended approval of an
updated dosing schedule for Libtayo (cemiplimab) covering all current cancer indications in the EU. The proposed regimen
would allow patients to transition to a 700mg intravenous dose every six weeks after an initial 12-week period of 350mg every
three weeks. CHMP based its recommendation on pharmacokinetic, pharmacodynamic and clinical data. The updated dosing
schedule may reduce treatment frequency and infusion times. The European Commission is expected to issue a final decision
in the coming months, which would extend the six-week dosing option to all approved cancer indications for Libtayo in the
EU.
Corporate Activity
Hansoh Pharmaceutical And Roche Sign Strategic Agreement On Antibody-Drug Conjugate
Development
Hansoh Pharmaceutical Group has entered into a strategic collaboration and licence agreement with Roche to develop a
portfolio of antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) therapies targeting multiple cancer types. Under the agreement, Hansoh will
oversee early-stage development for several ADC programmes, while Roche will manage global development and
commercialisation outside Mainland China; Hong Kong, China; Macao, China; and Taiwan, China, where Hansoh retains
commercial rights. The transaction includes an upfront payment of USD400mn, a first anniversary payment of USD110mn,
potential milestone payments exceeding USD15bn and royalties on future sales. Hansoh may also co-develop up to two
programmes outside Greater China, with the option to participate in US commercialisation. The agreement is intended to
facilitate the joint advancement of oncology therapeutics across multiple markets.
Vir Biotechnology And Gilead Sciences Announce Collaboration On T-Cell Engager Therapies For
Solid Tumours
Vir Biotechnology and Gilead Sciences have entered into a multi-programme collaboration and exclusive licence agreement
to develop three PRO-XTEN dual-masked T-cell engagers targeting antigens associated with solid tumours. The partnership
combines Vir’s artificial intelligence-enabled protein engineering and masking technology with Gilead’s expertise in oncology
development and commercialisation. The objective is to create immunotherapies with improved selectivity and safety for
treating solid tumours. Under the terms of the agreement, Vir will receive up to USD135mn in upfront and near-term
milestone payments, with the potential for an additional USD1.7bn in development, regulatory and commercial milestones as
well as royalties on future sales. This agreement reflects increasing industry interest in immuno-oncology and demonstrates
the application of Vir’s discovery platform in developing new therapeutic candidates.
Johnson & Johnson Reports Q3 2026 Sales Increase And Raises Year-End Guidance
Johnson & Johnson reported a 5.9% y-o-y increase in Q3 2026 sales to USD25.4bn, with growth across the Innovative
Medicine and MedTech segments. The company revised up its full-year outlook, now forecasting reported sales of
USD101.2bn and adjusted earnings per share of USD11.1. Key developments during the quarter included completing the
Firefly Bio acquisition, collaborating with Sail Biomedicines, and receiving regulatory approvals for IMAAVY in warm
autoimmune haemolytic anaemia and OTTAVA, a table-integrated soft-tissue robotic system. Operational sales in Innovative
Medicine rose by 7.9%, supported by oncology and immunology products, and MedTech growth was driven by cardiovascular
and orthopaedics demand. Johnson & Johnson continues to invest in portfolio expansion, targeting long-term growth.

Conferences
AstraZeneca To Present Phase III Oncology Trial Data Across Multiple Cancer Types At ESMO
2026
AstraZeneca will present data from four Phase III clinical trials at the ESMO 2026 Congress, covering gastric, lung, bladder and
breast cancers. The presentations include the first positive Phase III results for an anti-CLDN18.2 antibody-drug conjugate
(Sone-Ve) in advanced gastric cancer, as well as findings from combination regimens involving Imfinzi, Tagrisso and Orpathys
that demonstrate survival outcomes in bladder and lung cancers. More than 100 abstracts will highlight advances in
treatment modalities, precision medicine and combination therapies. These presentations aim to provide evidence for
potential treatment strategies and support AstraZeneca’s focus on improving outcomes across multiple tumour types.
Merck To Present Phase III Oncology Trial Results At ESMO Congress 2026, Expanding Cancer
Treatment Portfolio
Merck will present initial results from three Phase III oncology trials at the European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO)
Congress 2026, including data on intismeran autogene with Keytruda in adjuvant melanoma, sacituzumab tirumotecan for
advanced endometrial cancer, and Keytruda monotherapy in mismatch repair deficient endometrial cancer, all featured in
Presidential Symposia. Additional results from studies in non-small cell lung cancer and advanced renal cell carcinoma will
also be presented. These findings reflect Merck’s efforts to broaden its oncology portfolio with therapies that may improve
survival outcomes across several tumour types.
Eisai To Present Oncology Data At ESMO Congress 2026, Highlighting LENVIMA-Based Treatment
Outcomes In Advanced Cancer
Eisai will present new clinical data at the European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO) Congress 2026, with a focus on
outcomes from its oncology pipeline, particularly combination regimens involving LENVIMA (lenvatinib) for advanced renal
cell carcinoma and endometrial cancer. The company will report final results from Phase III LITESPARK-011 and
LITESPARK-012 studies, which investigated LENVIMA in combination with WELIREG and KEYTRUDA. These results support
recent FDA approvals and guideline recommendations for first-line therapies in renal cell carcinoma. Exploratory biomarker
findings from the Phase III LEAP-001 study contribute to the understanding of treatment strategies for advanced endometrial
cancer. Eisai will also provide patient survey data on quality of life and care preferences. The presentations offer updated
evidence for clinical practice and highlight ongoing collaboration with Merck in developing LENVIMA-based regimens.
